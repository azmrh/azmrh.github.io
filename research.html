Physiological Speed Control of Continuous-Flow LVADs

Undergraduate thesis, BUET Mechanical Engineering, 2025–2026. Supervisor: Dr. Sheikh Mohammad Shavik.

Continuous-flow left ventricular assist devices usually run at one clinician-set speed. They cannot follow the body's changing demand during exertion, and they flatten the arterial pulse. I built a coupled seven-chamber cardiovascular model with baroreflex feedback and a HeartMate III-class pump in Python. I then designed and benchmarked five closed-loop speed controllers against a fixed-speed baseline.

Controllers

  • Fuzzy Adaptive Sliding-Mode Controller (FASMC). A first-order sliding surface with a boundary layer against chattering. A nine-rule Mamdani fuzzy system adapts the switching gain online. The surface is $$s = \dot{e} + \lambda e$$ where $e$ is the cardiac-output tracking error.
  • Synchronized Pulsatile Controller (SPC). A systole-locked sinusoidal speed overlay on an adaptive FASMC base. This is the original contribution of the thesis.

Results

  • FASMC cut cardiac-output tracking error by 81.6% (IAE) and 93.5% (ISE) against fixed speed.
  • Across a 50-patient virtual population, FASMC held an interquartile range below 0.02 L/min (p < 0.001, Wilcoxon signed-rank).
  • SPC recovered a 12-fold higher pulsatility index at exercise, for a cardiac-output penalty of only 0.04 L/min.

Bimanual Mobile Manipulation of Large Deformable Objects

M.Sc. research, University of Dhaka, 2026–present. Supervisor: Dr. Shamim Ahmed Deowan.

Most cloth-folding robots work on towels and shirts that fit inside the reach of one fixed dual-arm platform. A bedsheet is about two metres on each side, so folding it needs two partners who walk. This project uses two independently mobile manipulators that coordinate to unfold and fold a bedsheet-scale cloth.

I am building the pipeline in simulation first, with ROS 2, Gazebo, MoveIt 2 and MuJoCo for cloth dynamics, before sim-to-real transfer on hardware.
